# Event data and managed collection competitors

Research date: 2026-11-26. This review covers six companies that overlap with Eventroam's festival catalog or its collection services. Customer evidence includes buyer publications, public contracts, and vendor case studies. Customer names in vendor case studies show reported use at the time of publication. This review has no common test set for coverage, accuracy, or update delay.

## Summary

Seeker has the closest offer for event calendars. It combines collection from agreed sources, review, and delivery to several publishers. JamBase has the closest data model for music festivals and links a stable series to its editions. Data Thistle has the clearest public example of a buyer paying for a maintained regional event dataset. [Calgary calendar announcement](https://www.avenuecalgary.com/things-to-do/the-scene-launches-new-calgary-events-calendar/) · [JamBase festival API specification](https://data.jambase.com/openapi.json) · [VisitScotland contract award](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/search/show/search_view.aspx?ID=NOV673874&catID=)

Eventroam maintains a worldwide festival catalog with a separate record for each edition. Its ingestion workflow uses source evidence and saved catalog context to review changes. It also offers data collection from agreed sources and development of systems for a client's own workflow. [Eventroam overview](../../README.md#product-idea) · [Ingestion process](../ingestion-process.md#process-at-a-glance) · [Service offer](../../src/features/project-presentation/project-presentation.tsx)

## Seeker Events Network

**Offer and positioning.** Seeker sells an updated local calendar to tourism organizations, publishers, and application teams. Its crawler collects events from partner websites and custom sources. Seeker combines duplicates and says it reviews records before delivery. Customers can use an embedded calendar or receive structured records through an API. Records include the event ID, dates, venue, organizer, and source attribution. [Events API](https://seeker.io/events/events-api/)

**Customers.** RedPoint Media's Avenue Calgary confirms that Seeker provides the shared event database for four Calgary partners. RedPoint, Tourism Calgary, Calgary Downtown Strategy, and Calgary Arts Development publish to their own audiences. The Scene's editorial team helps review listings. The San Francisco Peninsula also lists Seeker in its board materials and publishes a live calendar. [Calgary announcement](https://www.avenuecalgary.com/things-to-do/the-scene-launches-new-calgary-events-calendar/) · [The Scene calendar](https://thescenecalgary.com/calgary-events-calendar/) · [Peninsula board materials](https://assets.simpleviewinc.com/simpleview/image/upload/v1/clients/sanmateoca-redesign/Board_Meeting_Materials_9_18_25_5a15c68e-2ca9-4fd7-82da-ba65e2633c3a.pdf)

**Strengths and limits.** The Calgary case shows how one collection effort can support several publishers with separate editorial choices. Seeker supports recurring events with exceptions and added dates. Its published coverage is strongest in North American markets where it operates. Seeker says new listings can take 24 to 48 hours after discovery to go live, with changes and cancellations on the same review cycle. Public API materials describe record-level source attribution. They give no basis to compare a persistent annual festival series or evidence for individual fields with Eventroam. [Events API](https://seeker.io/events/events-api/) · [Recurring events](https://seeker.io/help/events/managing-events/creating-recurring-events/) · [Source review](https://seeker.io/help/events/getting-started/guide-to-submitting-event-sources/)

## JamBase Data

**Offer and positioning.** JamBase licenses a database of concerts, music festivals, artists, and venues through an API and bulk or custom feeds. It combines direct feeds from ticketing companies, promoters, venues, and festival organizers. Developers use it to add live music discovery, ticket links, and historical data to their products. [Data platform](https://data.jambase.com/) · [Uses by industry](https://data.jambase.com/solutions)

**Customers.** Denver's Regional Transportation District confirms that Next Ride uses JamBase to let riders search for concerts and plan trips to them. Artist Growth documents a JamBase integration that matches an artist by Spotify ID. These describe integrations. Their commercial terms are private. [Denver RTD announcement](https://www.rtd-denver.com/community/news/2024/rtd-website-next-ride-app-now-available-in-korean) · [Artist Growth integration](https://help.artistgrowth.com/en/jambase-integration)

**Strengths and limits.** The API gives each festival series a stable ID and each edition a separate event record. The archive can include daily lineups and billing order. The schema also supports rescheduled and cancelled events and includes the previous start date when available. JamBase depends on its suppliers and on matching artists and venues correctly. Its focus is live music. Historical access depends on the plan, and the standard commercial licence restricts redistribution of raw records. The public schema documents source identifiers and links. It gives no basis to compare the evidence that supports individual facts. [API specification](https://data.jambase.com/openapi.json) · [Outside Lands archive example](https://data.jambase.com/outsidellms) · [Coverage FAQ](https://data.jambase.com/faq) · [Pricing](https://data.jambase.com/pricing) · [Commercial terms](https://data.jambase.com/legal/commercial-terms)

## Data Thistle

**Offer and positioning.** Data Thistle sells UK live-event listings to publishers, tourism organizations, and teams that plan for demand. It offers an API, widgets, and custom feeds. It advertises custom feeds from £50 per month. Sources include venues, festival organizers, box offices, ticket sellers, and direct submissions. Data Thistle says most feed customers receive data daily. [Publisher products](https://www.datathistle.com/what-we-do/for-publishers/) · [Sources and delivery](https://www.datathistle.com/about/events-data/)

**Customers.** VisitScotland signed a £119,920 contract excluding VAT on 2 November 2026 for regular events content and a consolidated Scottish dataset. The amount is the full contract value. Renfrewshire Council also reports a paid partnership for daily updates to its Paisley listings. Data Thistle reports that TransPennine Express uses a daily feed to plan for travel demand. [VisitScotland contract award](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/search/show/search_view.aspx?ID=NOV673874&catID=) · [Renfrewshire evaluation](https://www1.renfrewshire.gov.uk/media/16961/Future-Paisley-evaluation-report-appendices/pdf/Future_Paisley_Evaluation_Report-Appendices.pdf?m=1734369361497) · [TransPennine Express case study](https://www.datathistle.com/success-stories/transpennine-express/)

**Strengths and limits.** Data Thistle matches records across sources and uses human review for conflicts. Its standard feed models individual performances with dates, prices, booking links, and cancellations. It focuses on the UK. Festival and series names are text fields in the standard feed. That feed delivers snapshots, and its schema gives no separate edition entity or field-level change history. For a festival with several locations, it can use a general venue label. These choices fit a local listings feed and limit the detail available for an edition archive. [Sources and quality checks](https://www.datathistle.com/about/events-data/) · [Standard feed specification](https://files.datathistle.com/feeds/docs/feedspec.pdf)

## PredictHQ

**Offer and positioning.** PredictHQ sells global event data and demand forecasts for pricing, staffing, inventory, and delivery planning. Its Events API supplies records with categories, locations, dates, and estimated attendance and spend. The Features API supplies daily or weekly inputs for forecasting models. [Product APIs](https://www.predicthq.com/apis) · [Features API](https://docs.predicthq.com/getting-started/core-concepts/what-is-the-features-api) · [Event database](https://www.predicthq.com/events/event-database)

**Customers.** Duetto confirms that it brings PredictHQ data into its revenue tools so hotel teams can see events and adjust rates. Hotel analytics provider Optimand documents its use of PredictHQ's event data and attendance estimates in demand analysis. [Duetto announcement](https://www.duettocloud.com/en-us/library/duetto-and-predicthq-empower-hotels-with-events-data) · [Optimand case study](https://www.optimand.com/case-studies/predicthq/)

**Strengths and limits.** The API groups recurring events and allows customers to synchronize changed and cancelled records. PredictHQ can forecast a future instance before organizers announce it, then change its status when it receives confirmation. This helps planning. Public listings need to filter by status. The standard event feed omits official event and ticket links. Its public schema describes event records and update times. The depth of coverage for small festivals and the evidence behind individual fields remain open. [Recurring events](https://docs.predicthq.com/getting-started/guides/date-and-time-guides/working-with-recurring-events) · [Predicted events](https://docs.predicthq.com/getting-started/predicthq-data/predicted-events) · [Update guide](https://docs.predicthq.com/integrations/integration-guides/keep-data-updated-via-api) · [Event database FAQ](https://www.predicthq.com/events/event-database)

## Zyte

**Offer and positioning.** Zyte Data delivers maintained feeds from agreed websites. The team agrees the schema and update frequency with the customer. AI builds the extraction and engineers review it before delivery. The service can deliver complete snapshots or changed records. Zyte gives an indicative starting price of about $500 per month for one source and about $200 per month for each additional source in the same feed. Price depends on sites, fields, frequency, and volume. Zyte also sells an extraction API and hosts customer-deployed scrapers in Scrapy Cloud. [Managed service and pricing](https://www.zyte.com/data-extraction/) · [Zyte products](https://docs.zyte.com/web-scraping/get-started.html)

**Customers.** Zyte targets teams that rely on web data for pricing, property, finance, travel, and other products. Its managed collection case describes weekly product and price feeds from hundreds of sites for an unnamed retailer operating across more than 30 countries. Its named cases for DebunkEU and Kinzen document use of the extraction API. [Managed retail case study](https://www.zyte.com/case-study/global-retailer-zyte-ai-scraping-llm/) · [DebunkEU case study](https://www.zyte.com/case-study/debunk-eu-scrapes-millions-of-news-articles-with-zyte/) · [Kinzen case study](https://www.zyte.com/case-study/kinzen-providing-structured-news-data-for-personalized-feeds-with-ai/)

**Strengths and limits.** Zyte agrees measures such as accuracy, completeness, and timeliness for each feed. It monitors runs and repairs extraction when a site changes. Its published terms assume sources without a login. A material site change can lead to a price change or extra development fees. Zyte can stop affected work if it becomes legally or technically infeasible. The published offer covers a customer-defined feed. Festival identity, edition history, and evidence for individual facts would need their own requirements. [Quality measures](https://www.zyte.com/data-extraction/) · [Data Services terms](https://www.zyte.com/terms-policies/terms-of-service/)

## Browse AI

**Offer and positioning.** Browse AI sells no-code website extraction and monitoring. Customers on self-service plans select the pages and fields, then schedule robots and send the output to other tools. On Premium, Browse AI builds and maintains the workflow, transforms and combines records, and delivers them on a schedule. Premium starts at $500 per month with annual billing. The service page advertises a 98% accuracy service level and states that each managed contract includes quality and delivery terms. The page does not define how it calculates the headline figure. [Plans and pricing](https://www.browse.ai/pricing) · [Managed service](https://www.browse.ai/service)

**Customers.** Automation agency Axiabits reports using Browse AI's self-service tools on client research projects. Its example uses a conference speaker list, and the article discloses affiliate links. Browse AI's managed-service cases describe legal technology, price monitoring, and vehicle certification. The buyers in those cases are unnamed. [Axiabits account](https://axiabits.com/how-to-scrape-leads-from-any-website-using-browse-ai-2026-guide/) · [Managed-service examples](https://www.browse.ai/service)

**Strengths and limits.** The standard tool supports scheduled runs, failure alerts, run history with origin URLs, and several ways to export data. A major site change can require a customer to retrain a robot. The standard tool has limits with virtual lists, direct interaction with embedded frames, and multi-factor authentication. Run history records what each robot returned. A maintained festival catalog with stable edition records and evidence for individual facts would need a separate design. [Run history and export](https://help.browse.ai/en/articles/10477537-how-to-view-manage-and-export-your-scraped-data) · [Site changes](https://help.browse.ai/en/articles/12271136-does-browse-ai-support-websites-that-change-frequently) · [Site support](https://help.browse.ai/en/articles/10452902-what-websites-work-with-browse-ai)

## Implications for Eventroam

- **A maintained regional feed has a demonstrated buyer.** Seeker's Calgary deployment shows a shared collection process that serves several publishers. The VisitScotland award shows direct spending on the same broad need. These cases make tourism organizations and destination publishers relevant buyers for a scoped Eventroam offer. One award gives a contract value for one buyer and region. [Calgary deployment](https://www.avenuecalgary.com/things-to-do/the-scene-launches-new-calgary-events-calendar/) · [VisitScotland award](https://www.sell2wales.gov.wales/search/show/search_view.aspx?ID=NOV673874&catID=)
- **A separate festival edition is already present in the market.** JamBase has a festival series and edition archive. PredictHQ links recurring event records with event groups. A useful festival comparison would test coverage across genres and formats, how soon confirmed changes appear, and how well each service can show the source for a disputed date, venue, or admission rule. [JamBase API specification](https://data.jambase.com/openapi.json) · [PredictHQ recurrence model](https://docs.predicthq.com/getting-started/guides/date-and-time-guides/working-with-recurring-events) · [Eventroam ingestion](../ingestion-process.md)
- **Ongoing support is part of the paid offer.** Zyte and Browse AI publish a starting price for managed extraction and agree delivery requirements per customer. Zyte's terms make significant changes to source sites a possible cost for the buyer. For Eventroam, this gives a basis for scoping a pilot by sources, fields, update interval, and the work needed to resolve exceptions. [Zyte service](https://www.zyte.com/data-extraction/) · [Zyte terms](https://www.zyte.com/terms-policies/terms-of-service/) · [Browse AI Premium](https://www.browse.ai/pricing)
