# Conference market research for Eventroam

Research conducted: 2026-09-30. Scope: worldwide discovery of IT, business, and academic conferences. This is desk research, not a traffic study or revenue forecast.

## Market and competitors

Conferences are a large commercial category: the Events Industry Council estimates **$383.2 billion in 2025 direct spending on conventions, conferences, and congresses**. That figure covers the events themselves, not the addressable revenue of a directory. [EIC and Oxford Economics](https://news.eventscouncil.org/eic-releases-full-2026-global-economic-significance-of-business-events-study/).

The market is already served by broad directories such as [10times](https://login.10times.com/plans) and [Clocate](https://www.clocate.com/), a specialist IT directory in [dev.events](https://dev.events/), and academic discovery through [Conference Alerts](https://conferencealerts.com/help). Registration and organizer tools, including [Bizzabo](https://www.bizzabo.com/pricing), compete for related budgets. **Our product hypothesis** is that a generic global list would have little distinct value, while a carefully verified guide to in-person conferences worth traveling to could offer comparable dates, location, subject, access rules, and official registration links.

## Monetization prospects

Paid organizer promotion has the clearest early precedent. Conference Alerts lists a **$55** highlighted listing, **$160** email alert, and **$320 per month** homepage placement; 10times sells prominent placement and leads starting at **$5 per lead**. These are competitor prices, not attainable prices for Eventroam. [Conference Alerts](https://conferencealerts.com/help); [10times](https://login.10times.com/plans).

Travel referrals could suit destination conferences: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/affiliate-program/v2/index.en-gb.html) pays for qualified partner bookings. **Our assessment** is that ticket referrals are less dependable as a core model because registration is fragmented across organizers and platforms. Lead sales, organizer software, and data subscriptions require proven audience, consent or data rights, and more sales and support work. Festival ticket referrals also have a working precedent: [Skiddle](https://www.skiddle.com/affiliates/) advertises affiliate earnings of up to **£4 per summer-festival ticket**.

## Operating difficulty and comparison with festivals

The existing Event and Occurrence model fits recurring conference series, but the catalog would need to distinguish public, invitation-only, academic, trade-show, hybrid, and online formats. Dates, venues, registration terms, and prices change; academic listings add legitimacy checks. [Google's event guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/event) requires accurate event pages and notes limits on its event search feature; [Think. Check. Attend.](https://thinkcheckattend.org/check/) documents checks for academic conferences. Source access is another constraint: [Eventbrite's terms](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/251210/eventbrite-terms-of-service/) prohibit automated extraction from its site.

**Assessment:** conferences may have greater revenue per organizer, while festivals are a better fit for Eventroam's current map and travel audience and are already the defined first-release scope. The conference advantage is a hypothesis until traffic, referrals, organizer demand, and the cost of verifying each record are measured.

## Recommendation

Finish the festival release, then test a separate, narrow conference catalog: around 100–150 in-person European events across IT and one non-IT industry, sourced from official organizer pages. Measure verification and refresh effort, outbound registration clicks, and organizer interest in clearly labeled paid promotion before expanding the scope.
