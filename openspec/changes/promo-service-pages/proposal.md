# Proposal

## Why

The project page offers data collection and custom development. A buyer needs a concrete example to decide which service fits their work.

## What Changes

- Give each service its own page and use the home page to introduce both services and the festival catalog.
- Give authors a text template for the overview and service cases.
- Use the project email address for contact.

## Requirements

- Visitors can open and share a page about data for a product or a page about custom agent pipelines for data processing.
- Each page explains one distinct case, its result, and what to send to start a discussion.
- Readers can tell which claims come from festival sources and which describe an example.
- Visitors can reach the live festival catalog and contact the project by email.
- Authors can use the text template to write another case.

## Acceptance criteria

- From the home page, open each service and return to the overview.
- The homepage shows which sources support a real festival update, what the team checked, and what changed in the published edition.
- The data page shows a published festival edition and how buyers can use edition data in calendars, trip planners, and festival guides.
- The pipeline page shows a possible workflow outside the events sector and a live case from the catalog.
- The contact links on all three pages name the action and address hello@eventroam.org.
- The template includes prompts for evidence, result, and contact.

## Impact

The change affects the public project site, its search listings, and contact link reports.
